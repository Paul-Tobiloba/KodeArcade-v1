# Concept Validation Guide

Status: prepared for use; no participants have been interviewed and no findings are claimed.

## Purpose

Find out how beginners encounter difficulty, what helps them continue, and which device and language constraints should shape KodeArcade. These conversations provide early design evidence; 3–5 sessions cannot establish learning effectiveness or represent every learner aged 8–14.

## Participants and setup

Aim for 3–5 relevant people, including beginner learners and, where possible, a parent, teacher, or mentor. If only adults are available, label their comments as adult perspectives and leave learner usability untested.

Allow about 15 minutes per conversation. For sessions with children, arrange guardian permission and the child's willingness to participate. Explain that they can skip questions or stop. Use participant codes such as P01 in notes; omit names, school names, contact details, and recordings. Do not commit identifiable research notes to the project repository.

Opening script: “I'm planning a coding learning activity and want to understand what learning something new is like for you. There are no right answers. You can skip any question. Today we're discussing an idea, so there isn't a working app to test yet.”

## Interview prompts

Ask about experience before describing the product. For adults, ask for specific examples they have observed and mark second-hand accounts clearly.

1. Tell me about the last time you tried to learn something on a phone or computer. What were you trying to do?
2. Where did you begin, and how did you decide what to try next?
3. Tell me about a time you got stuck. What did you do? What happened afterward?
4. What kind of explanation or help made a difference? Can you give an example?
5. Have you stopped an activity and returned later? What helped you remember where you were?
6. What device do you usually use? Is it shared? What happens when the connection is unavailable?
7. What makes on-screen instructions easier or harder to follow? Which language would you prefer for explanations?
8. Have you tried coding before? What did you make or try, and what was confusing?

Avoid suggesting that games, rewards, hints, or recommendations must be useful. Follow up with “What happened next?” or “Can you show or describe an example?”

## Concept reaction

After the experience questions, read this neutral description:

“KodeArcade is an idea for learning coding by arranging instructions to move a robot. You would run your instructions, see what happens, and change them. You could ask for hints, save your place, and see a suggested activity to try next.”

- In your own words, what do you think you would do here?
- Which part is unclear or might be frustrating?
- What would you expect when selecting “Hint”? What does “Power-Up” mean to you?
- If the activity suggested more practice, what would you expect to be able to choose next?
- What would be missing for you to use this on your usual device?

Record opinions about the idea separately from examples of actual past behaviour. Interest in the description is not evidence that the app is usable or improves learning.

## Assumptions to investigate

| Assumption | Evidence to seek | Possible design response |
|---|---|---|
| Getting stuck contributes to stopping | Specific accounts of where and why an activity ended | Prioritize the type of feedback that addresses the reported obstacle |
| Gradual hints are useful | Examples of helpful and unhelpful assistance | Revise hint wording; test actual recovery in the prototype |
| Returning after interruption is difficult | Accounts of losing place or forgetting instructions | Test a recap with saved work |
| A suggested next step is understandable | Participant explains the suggestion and available choices | Simplify wording and make alternatives visible |
| The interface fits available devices | Reported screen, input, sharing, and connection constraints | Choose the first test device and offline scenario accordingly |
| Game vocabulary supports understanding | Participant interprets “Mission”, “Hint”, and “Power-Up” | Prefer plain labels where meaning is unclear |

All assumptions above remain untested until evidence is recorded. Hint counts alone should not be treated as proof of low ability; people may ask for help for different reasons.

## Synthesis after the sessions

For each finding, record participant codes, supporting examples, contradictory evidence, and the resulting design decision. Report counts such as “2 of 4 participants mentioned losing their place” rather than broad claims about all learners. Keep assumptions and unanswered questions visible.
