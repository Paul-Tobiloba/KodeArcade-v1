# Learner Pathway

## Core experience

```text
Welcome
   ↓
Choose profile and accessibility preferences
   ↓
Two-minute skill check or "start from the beginning"
   ↓
Recommended starting mission
   ↓
Learn → Build → Run → Reflect
   ↓
Need help? Progressive hints and a simpler example
   ↓
Mission result and concept mastery update
   ↓
Review ← Recommendation → Practise → Advance
   ↓
Milestone mini-project
   ↓
Next-world and opportunity pathway
```

## Stage 1: Welcome and safe setup

The learner selects a nickname, avatar, preferred text size, sound setting, motion setting, and language. The proof of concept does not require a child's real name, email address, photograph, or precise location.

The learner can choose either:

- **Find my starting point:** a short, low-pressure diagnostic.
- **Start from the beginning:** no diagnostic required.

## Stage 2: First win

The first mission should take approximately three to five minutes and introduce one action at a time. Its purpose is to teach the interaction model and provide an achievable success experience.

Example mission: arrange movement blocks to help a character reach a destination.

The learner sees the program execute step by step and can replay it without penalty.

## Stage 3: Supported practice

Each mission follows the same predictable rhythm:

1. **Goal:** a short explanation of what must be achieved.
2. **Try:** the learner arranges or edits blocks.
3. **Run:** the program executes visually.
4. **Feedback:** the platform identifies where execution stopped or diverged.
5. **Hint:** optional, progressive support is available.
6. **Reflect:** one short question reinforces the concept.

### Progressive hint ladder

- **Hint 1 — Notice:** points attention to the relevant part of the problem.
- **Hint 2 — Concept:** reminds the learner of the programming idea involved.
- **Hint 3 — Example:** shows a smaller analogous example.
- **Hint 4 — Guided step:** helps place the next block without revealing the entire solution.

Using hints does not remove points or shame the learner. Help-seeking is treated as a normal learning behaviour.

## Stage 4: Adaptive next step

The proof of concept uses explainable rules rather than a black-box score.

| Observation | Recommendation | Learner-facing explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Program meets the mission goal, with any amount of help | Advance or replay | “Your program reached its goal. You can continue, or replay to explore another solution.” |
| Program does not meet the goal | Practise | “Try one small change, then run again.” |
| Three or more unsuccessful runs in a later mission | Optional review | “A simpler route may help. You can also keep working here.” |
| Long interruption | Resume recap | “Here is what you completed last time and what comes next.” |

The learner can accept or override the recommendation. These are provisional support rules, not validated ability measures. Puzzle completion is not proof of conceptual mastery; hint use does not reduce the learner's progress.

## Stage 5: Milestone project

After the four sprint missions, the learner completes a rescue-route project with more than one valid solution using sequences and simple loops. Conditions are reserved for the expanded world after the sprint.

The project demonstrates transfer: the learner applies concepts rather than merely repeating a known pattern.

## Stage 6: Pathway and opportunity

After the mini-project, the learner sees:

- skills demonstrated in plain language;
- a recommended next learning world;
- one offline activity requiring no computer;
- one curated extension resource with source and data-size information;
- examples of what people create with the relevant skills.

The platform should avoid promising a career outcome from a short course. Its role is to make the next step visible and achievable.

## Return and retention loop

KodeArcade supports healthy engagement rather than pressure:

- Progress is saved after every meaningful action.
- The home screen answers “Where was I?” and “What should I do next?”
- Streaks are optional and can be restored through learning activity.
- Learners receive milestone celebrations, not endless notifications.
- Missed days are not framed as failure.
